> **Unpublished working paper**, prepared during postgraduate study at the Doha Institute for Graduate Studies. The full text is in Arabic and available as a PDF from the top of this page.

## Framing

Unemployment reflects an imbalance between labour supply and demand. Rates rise in developing economies when productive sectors cannot generate jobs at the required scale, pushing labour toward services as a last resort — yet services, mismatched to workers' qualifications, cannot absorb them.

The analysis applies to Palestine, but **political factors carry the greatest weight** in weakening the economic structure and the resources needed to create employment, after long years of blockade and political division.

## Characteristics

- Severe concentration among young people aged **15–24**, particularly university graduates
- Youth unemployment rose from **20% in 2007** to **44.7% in 2017**, easing slightly to **42% in 2020**
- The rate reached **73% among young women** in the same age group in 2020
- High prevalence among holders of academic qualifications (intermediate diploma and above)

## Focus

The paper examines the role of **small and medium-sized enterprises** as a route to addressing the problem, and the conditions required for that role to work.
