> **Capstone research** for the MA in Development Economics — School of Economics, Administration and Public Policy, Doha Institute for Graduate Studies. Not published in a peer-reviewed journal. The full text is in Arabic and available from the top of this page.

## Research question

Agriculture's share in Gaza has fallen across major economic indicators — output, employment and exports — for familiar reasons: higher wages and profits outside the sector, less physically demanding work elsewhere, the seasonality of farm labour, population growth against shrinking cultivated area, poverty among farmers, and scarce irrigation water with the production costs that follow.

The study asks a further question: **did Israeli bombardment compound the effect of climate change itself on the sector?**

## Method

The study is econometric, at governorate level, using:

- The **SPEI drought index** at 3-month and 6-month accumulation periods as the measure of climate stress
- The **NDVI vegetation index** as a proxy for vegetation cover and agricultural condition
- Data on **agricultural land damaged by bombardment**, disaggregated by means of attack
- Mean temperature and rainfall data by governorate

Marginal effects of 3- and 6-month drought conditions on NDVI were estimated.

## Findings

- Gaza's governorates experience climate-change effects **to differing degrees**.
- There is an **inverse relationship** between the drought index and the vegetation index: as drought intensifies, vegetated area contracts.
- **Israeli bombardment of agricultural land compounded the effect of climate change** and contributed independently to the contraction of vegetation cover.
