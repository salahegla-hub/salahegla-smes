## Abstract

This paper analyses the state of the agricultural economy in the Gaza Strip as a pillar of food security and of economic and social development under conditions of occupation and blockade.

It starts from the problem of **an inability to convert latent agricultural potential into a resilient development lever**, given compounding geographic, political and environmental constraints, fragmented landholdings, and the failure of uniform agricultural policy.

## Method

The study uses **descriptive analysis** of aggregate agricultural indicators — economic, geographic and spatial — with statistical comparisons centred on the **2010 and 2021 censuses**.

## Findings

**Shifting production patterns:** declining commitment to long-cycle cultivation (fruit trees and cattle) in favour of quick-return activity (vegetables and poultry) — a sign of **fragile agricultural investment** and a forced response to the absence of stability rather than a free economic choice.

**Spatial disparities:** geographic analysis reveals differences between Gaza's governorates in crop type, holding patterns and market integration — evidence of **no regional policy** that accounts for spatial specificity.

**Diagnosis:** the sector's challenges are not temporary obstacles but **structural problems**, requiring a re-reading of the agricultural-economic approach that combines quantitative and qualitative analysis, activates **decentralised agricultural policy** attentive to soil, water and security conditions, and secures rights of tenure and production.

## Conclusion

The study concludes that developing Gaza's agricultural sector is a **strategic entry point for economic cohesion**, requiring integrated coordination between government bodies, civil society and international organisations.
